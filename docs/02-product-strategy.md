# 02 — Product Strategy

> Canon: C-1, C-2, C-11, C-21, C-24 · v1.0 · 2026-07-11

## Market reality (from field intelligence)

- The auction-app category is **already crowded** (7+ tools). Feature lists are a losing game; every competitor has "auction, teams, budget."
- **CricHeroes is the giant** — free season management, enormous distribution, but no native, trustworthy live auction. Our wedge must be the thing they don't have: a flawless, dispute-proof, premium auction night.
- **Organizers pay per event, not per month.** Community cricket has no appetite for subscriptions; ₹500–₹5,000 per tournament is a natural, defensible price point (C-11).
- **The moat is trust, not features.** Anyone can clone a bid button. Nobody can clone three seasons of undisputed money records and the reputation that follows.

## Positioning

DesiAuction is the **trusted transaction and decision layer** for community sport (carries ratified decision D-004). Competitors sell software; we sell *the guarantee that the night goes right and the money is beyond question*.

## The wedge → expand sequence

1. **Wedge: the auction night.** One organizer, one Pass, one flawless evening witnessed by 200 people — every spectator is a prospect who just watched the product work.
2. **Expand: the tournament.** Squads, receipts, and results already live here; running the season here is the path of least resistance.
3. **Compound: the organization.** Associations and repeat organizers accumulate history, venues, player identities — switching costs made of their own trusted records (never ransomed, C-11).

## Growth model

- **The auction is the demo.** Stage and Overlay (C-3) are public, branded, and beautiful — distribution is built into the product's most public moment.
- **Player-side pull:** every registered player gets a dignified profile and a shareable sold-moment card; hundreds of players per tournament carry the brand into their networks.
- **No sales team at grassroots** (invariant 27): public pricing, self-serve passes. Associations get a partnerships motion later (H3), not a gate.

## Monetization

| Tier | Who | What |
|------|-----|------|
| **Free** | Gully/first-timers | Full trust spine (immutable bids, receipts, dignity rules — trust is never a premium feature), limited teams/players, DesiAuction branding on public surfaces |
| **Pro Pass** (per tournament) | Serious organizers | Full limits, custom branding, Overlay, exports, WhatsApp notifications |
| **Association bundle** | Leagues/associations | Pass bundles, delegation (grants at org scope), multi-tournament reporting |

Payments via Razorpay (C-18). Registration-fee collection from players is a later revenue lane — only after the money spine has earned that right.

## Competitive rules of engagement

- Never compete on feature count; compete on **certainty and occasion**.
- Never build engagement mechanics to inflate usage (C-19); our usage is real nights that really happened.
- Interoperate rather than lock in (public API, exports, C-14) — openness is a trust signal that giants can't cheaply imitate.

## Strategic risks and answers

| Risk | Answer |
|------|--------|
| CricHeroes ships an auction | Our auction must be *years* better on night-of experience and money trust, not weeks better |
| Price race to zero | Free tier already gives away the commodity part; the Pass sells the occasion and the guarantee |
| Single-event seasonality | H2 season layer converts one night into a multi-month relationship |
| Trust is slow to prove | Every completed auction emits public, verifiable artifacts (results pages, receipts) — proof accumulates by design |
